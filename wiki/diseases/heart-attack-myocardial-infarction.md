# Heart Attack / Myocardial Infarction

> ⚠️ **Educational reference only.** This wiki does not diagnose conditions and does not prescribe treatment. If severe warning signs are present (chest pain, stroke-like symptoms, severe breathing difficulty, fainting, severe allergic reaction, uncontrolled bleeding, seizure, or confusion with illness), seek urgent medical care.

## Overview
A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked or severely reduced. It is treated as a time-sensitive medical emergency because heart muscle can be damaged when oxygen supply is interrupted.

## Common Symptoms
- chest pain or pressure
- pain spreading to left arm, shoulder, jaw, neck, or back
- shortness of breath
- sweating
- nausea or vomiting
- dizziness
- unusual fatigue

## Risk Factors
- high blood pressure
- high cholesterol
- smoking
- diabetes
- obesity
- older age
- family history of heart disease
- physical inactivity

## Warning Signs
- crushing chest pain
- chest pain with sweating
- chest pain with shortness of breath
- fainting or near-fainting
- pain spreading to arm, jaw, neck, or back

## Common Investigations
- [[ECG]]
- troponin blood test
- blood pressure
- oxygen saturation
- chest X-ray
- basic blood tests

## Commonly Involved Departments
- [[Emergency Medicine]]
- [[Cardiology]]
- Intensive Care Unit
- Cardiac Catheterization Lab

## Related or Overlapping Conditions
- angina
- aortic dissection
- pulmonary embolism
- [[GERD / Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease]]
- panic attack
- pneumonia
- muscle strain

## Typical Hospital Flow
A patient with suspicious chest pain is usually assessed quickly. The early focus is on vital signs, ECG, cardiac blood markers, oxygen status, and deciding whether cardiology escalation is needed.
