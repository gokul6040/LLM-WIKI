# Hospital Disease Reference

## Important Note

This file is a general educational reference about common diseases, symptoms, warning signs, investigations, and hospital department routing. It is not a substitute for medical care, diagnosis, or treatment. Real symptoms should be discussed with a licensed healthcare professional. Severe symptoms such as chest pain, stroke-like symptoms, severe breathing difficulty, fainting, severe allergic reaction, uncontrolled bleeding, seizure, or confusion with illness require urgent medical attention.

## How This File Is Organized

The file is organized around practical hospital reasoning:

- disease profiles
- symptom relationships
- warning signs
- common investigations
- department routing
- overlapping conditions

A symptom can connect to many diseases. A test can support an evaluation pathway but does not automatically confirm a disease. Department routing depends on severity, age, risk factors, pregnancy status, vital signs, examination, and test results.

# 1. Major Disease Groups

## Cardiovascular Diseases

Cardiovascular diseases affect the heart and blood vessels. Important examples include heart attack, angina, stroke, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, and heart failure. Warning signs often include chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, sudden weakness, speech difficulty, or very high blood pressure with symptoms.

## Respiratory Diseases

Respiratory diseases affect the airways, lungs, and oxygen exchange. Important examples include pneumonia, asthma, COPD, tuberculosis, pulmonary embolism, flu-like illness, and lung cancer. Warning signs often include low oxygen, severe breathlessness, blue lips, chest pain with breathing, confusion, or coughing blood.

## Infectious Diseases

Infections can be local or systemic. Some infections remain mild, while others can lead to sepsis or organ dysfunction. Fever should be interpreted with other features such as cough, rash, travel, stiff neck, confusion, low blood pressure, abdominal pain, urinary symptoms, or breathing difficulty.

## Endocrine and Metabolic Diseases

Endocrine and metabolic diseases include diabetes, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycemia, and thyroid disorders. These conditions can cause fatigue, weight changes, thirst, urination changes, palpitations, confusion, or coma in severe cases.

## Kidney and Urinary Diseases

Kidney and urinary conditions include chronic kidney disease, acute kidney injury, urinary tract infection, kidney stones, and complications from diabetes or hypertension. Warning signs include reduced urination, swelling, confusion, severe flank pain with fever, or abnormal electrolytes.

## Gastrointestinal and Liver Diseases

Gastrointestinal diseases include appendicitis, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, ulcers, GERD, IBD, cirrhosis, and hepatitis. Red flags include severe abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, vomiting blood, black stools, jaundice with severe illness, persistent vomiting, or weight loss.

## Cancer

Cancer can affect many organs. Warning signs are often persistent and progressive rather than sudden: unexplained weight loss, persistent lump, unusual bleeding, coughing blood, blood in stool or urine, change in bowel habits, non-healing wound, or persistent pain.

## Blood and Immune Diseases

Blood and immune conditions include anemia, leukemia, lymphoma, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, and severe allergic reactions. Symptoms may include fatigue, bleeding, bruising, infections, joint swelling, rash, fever, or multi-system involvement.

## Mental Health and Cognitive Conditions

Depression, anxiety, panic attacks, psychosis, and dementia can affect mood, behavior, memory, and physical symptoms. These conditions can overlap with medical diseases such as thyroid disease, anemia, heart rhythm problems, stroke, infection, and substance-related conditions.

## 1. Heart Attack / Myocardial Infarction

**Disease group:** Cardiovascular

### Overview

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked or severely reduced. It is treated as a time-sensitive medical emergency because heart muscle can be damaged when oxygen supply is interrupted.

### Common Symptoms

- chest pain or pressure
- pain spreading to left arm, shoulder, jaw, neck, or back
- shortness of breath
- sweating
- nausea or vomiting
- dizziness
- unusual fatigue

### Risk Factors

- high blood pressure
- high cholesterol
- smoking
- diabetes
- obesity
- older age
- family history of heart disease
- physical inactivity

### Warning Signs

- crushing chest pain
- chest pain with sweating
- chest pain with shortness of breath
- fainting or near-fainting
- pain spreading to arm, jaw, neck, or back

### Common Investigations

- ECG
- troponin blood test
- blood pressure
- oxygen saturation
- chest X-ray
- basic blood tests

### Commonly Involved Departments

- Emergency Medicine
- Cardiology
- Intensive Care Unit
- Cardiac Catheterization Lab

### Related or Overlapping Conditions

- angina
- aortic dissection
- pulmonary embolism
- GERD
- panic attack
- pneumonia
- muscle strain

### Typical Hospital Flow

A patient with suspicious chest pain is usually assessed quickly. The early focus is on vital signs, ECG, cardiac blood markers, oxygen status, and deciding whether cardiology escalation is needed.

## 2. Angina

**Disease group:** Cardiovascular

### Overview

Angina is chest discomfort caused by reduced blood flow to the heart muscle. It may occur during exertion or emotional stress and improve with rest, but new or worsening angina can represent an emergency pattern.

### Common Symptoms

- chest tightness
- chest pressure
- shortness of breath
- pain with exertion
- fatigue
- pain spreading to arm, jaw, or back

### Risk Factors

- coronary artery disease
- high blood pressure
- diabetes
- smoking
- high cholesterol
- older age

### Warning Signs

- chest pain at rest
- new chest pain
- worsening frequency
- chest pain with sweating
- chest pain with fainting

### Common Investigations

- ECG
- troponin if acute
- stress test when stable
- echocardiogram
- lipid profile

### Commonly Involved Departments

- Emergency Medicine
- Cardiology
- Primary Care

### Related or Overlapping Conditions

- heart attack
- GERD
- panic attack
- aortic dissection
- pulmonary embolism

### Typical Hospital Flow

The key distinction is whether the pattern is stable or unstable. New, worsening, or rest pain is usually treated as a higher-risk chest pain pathway.

### Common Investigations

## 12. Sepsis

**Disease group:** Systemic / Infectious Emergency

### Overview

Sepsis is a dangerous body-wide response to infection that can lead to organ dysfunction, shock, and death. It is treated as a medical emergency.

### Common Symptoms

- fever or low temperature
- fast heart rate
- fast breathing
- low blood pressure
- confusion
- reduced urination
- weakness
- clammy skin

### Risk Factors

- older age
- very young age
- weakened immune system
- diabetes
- chronic kidney disease
- recent surgery
- urinary catheter
- recent hospitalization

### Warning Signs

- infection signs with confusion
- infection signs with low blood pressure
- fast breathing
- reduced urination
- cold or clammy skin
- severe weakness

### Common Investigations

- vital signs
- blood cultures
- complete blood count
- lactate
- kidney function
- liver function
- urine test
- source-specific imaging

### Commonly Involved Departments

- Emergency Medicine
- Internal Medicine
- Infectious Disease
- Intensive Care Unit
- Surgery if source control is needed

### Related or Overlapping Conditions

- pneumonia
- urinary tract infection
- meningitis
- abdominal infection
- skin infection
- malaria
- dengue

### Typical Hospital Flow

The pathway recognizes infection plus danger signs, checks organ function and circulation, identifies the likely infection source, and escalates if shock or organ failure is suspected.

# 7. Cross-Disease Reasoning Notes

## Fever Is Nonspecific

Fever can occur in many conditions, including pneumonia, influenza, dengue, malaria, sepsis, urinary tract infection, meningitis, appendicitis, cellulitis, and viral illnesses. Fever becomes more concerning when combined with confusion, stiff neck, low blood pressure, severe shortness of breath, rash with severe illness, severe abdominal pain, or persistent vomiting.

## Chest Pain Requires Careful Separation

Chest pain can come from heart disease, lungs, stomach acid reflux, muscles, anxiety, blood clots, or aortic disease. Dangerous patterns include chest pain with sweating, breathlessness, fainting, radiation to the arm or jaw, sudden tearing quality, or major cardiovascular risk factors.

## Shortness of Breath Has Many Body-System Causes

Shortness of breath may come from lung disease, heart disease, blood disorders, allergic reactions, infection, anxiety, metabolic problems, or blood clots. Severe breathlessness, low oxygen, blue lips, chest pain, confusion, or airway swelling increases urgency.

## Chronic Cough Changes the Pattern

A short cough with fever may suggest a respiratory infection. A cough lasting weeks with weight loss, night sweats, or coughing blood raises concern for tuberculosis, lung cancer, chronic lung disease, or other persistent respiratory disease.

## Confusion Is a Major Red Flag

Confusion can occur with stroke, sepsis, meningitis, low blood glucose, dehydration, kidney failure, liver failure, medication toxicity, head injury, or dementia. New confusion, especially with fever or neurological symptoms, is usually treated as urgent.

## Abdominal Pain Needs Context

Abdominal pain should be interpreted by location, duration, severity, vomiting, fever, bowel changes, urinary symptoms, pregnancy status, and whether the abdomen is rigid or the patient is fainting. Appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney stone, ulcers, and gastroenteritis may overlap.

## Weight Loss Is Important When Persistent and Unexplained

Unexplained weight loss can connect to cancer, tuberculosis, HIV, hyperthyroidism, inflammatory bowel disease, diabetes, depression, chronic infection, and other chronic diseases. The associated symptoms and duration matter.

## Risk Factors Modify Interpretation

The same symptom can mean different things depending on age, pregnancy, diabetes, immune status, heart disease, kidney disease, smoking history, travel history, medication use, and previous medical history.

# 8. Compact Condition-to-Symptom Map


## Heart Attack / Myocardial Infarction

**Main symptoms:** chest pain or pressure, pain spreading to left arm, shoulder, jaw, neck, or back, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or vomiting, dizziness

**Departments:** Emergency Medicine, Cardiology, Intensive Care Unit, Cardiac Catheterization Lab

**Main symptoms:** chest tightness, chest pressure, shortness of breath, pain with exertion, fatigue, pain spreading to arm, jaw, or back  
**Major warning signs:** chest pain at rest, new chest pain, worsening frequency, chest pain with sweating

**Main symptoms:** sudden face drooping, sudden arm or leg weakness, sudden one-sided numbness, speech difficulty, confusion, vision problems

## Which diseases are connected to chest pain?

Chest pain is connected to heart attack, angina, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, GERD, panic attack, and muscle strain. The most urgent associated features are sweating, shortness of breath, fainting, pain spreading to arm or jaw, sudden tearing pain, or very high-risk cardiovascular history.

## Which diseases are connected to fever and cough?

Fever and cough commonly connect to pneumonia, influenza, COVID-like illness, tuberculosis, and other respiratory infections. If cough is chronic with weight loss and night sweats, tuberculosis or cancer-related patterns become more important. If oxygen is low or confusion is present, the situation becomes more urgent.

## Why is fever with confusion serious?

Fever with confusion can connect to sepsis, meningitis, severe dehydration, malaria with neurological involvement, low blood glucose, or other serious systemic conditions. It is important because it may suggest the brain, circulation, oxygen level, or body-wide infection response is affected.

## What diseases are connected to shortness of breath?

Shortness of breath can connect to asthma, COPD, pneumonia, heart failure, pulmonary embolism, heart attack, anaphylaxis, anemia, sepsis, and panic attack. Low oxygen, blue lips, chest pain, fainting, confusion, or airway swelling are high-priority warning signs.

## Which conditions are connected to unexplained weight loss?

Unexplained weight loss can connect to cancer, tuberculosis, HIV, diabetes, hyperthyroidism, inflammatory bowel disease, depression, chronic infection, and chronic organ disease. Persistent symptoms, bleeding, chronic cough, fever, night sweats, or lumps increase concern.

## Which symptoms suggest stroke?

Stroke warning signs include sudden face drooping, sudden arm or leg weakness, sudden one-sided numbness, speech difficulty, confusion, trouble seeing, trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance, or sudden severe headache.

## Which diseases connect to vomiting?

Vomiting can connect to gastroenteritis, diabetic ketoacidosis, appendicitis, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, migraine, meningitis, pregnancy-related conditions, dengue warning signs, kidney disease, and medication or toxin exposure. Persistent vomiting, blood in vomit, dehydration, confusion, severe abdominal pain, or pregnancy makes it more concerning.

## Which departments handle breathing problems?

Severe breathing problems commonly start with Emergency Medicine. Pulmonology may be involved for asthma, COPD, pneumonia complications, tuberculosis, chronic cough, and lung cancer suspicion. Cardiology may be involved when heart failure, heart attack, or rhythm problems are possible. Allergy and Immunology may be involved for anaphylaxis follow-up.
